Operations Manual

A practical guide for operating a Wild Ledger community. Start with the job in front of you. Open the deeper pieces only when they help.

Welcome Aboard

Wild Ledger is a LEAP distribution engine. We give you the tools to build a community, reward participation, and distribute LEAP through utility.

But before you get started, understand a few things.

Not your keys. Not your community.

Your Operating Wallet controls your community. Protect it. If you lose access to that wallet, you lose the ability to operate your community. Wild Ledger doesn't hold your keys, and nobody is coming to rescue you.

Your Community ID and Community Operating Wallet address are permanently bound together. Operating authority cannot be transferred to a different XRPL wallet address, even while you still control the original wallet. Protect access to that permanent wallet.

We'd like to know less about you.

We don't need to know who you are, where you live, or what you had for breakfast. We need a wallet address and the ledger evidence necessary to make the system work.

Your business is your business.

We don't manage communities. We enable them.

Create a community. Give people a reason to participate. Distribute LEAP. Build something worthwhile.

Some communities will thrive. Others will go quiet. That's how it works.

Wild Ledger doesn't promise to keep your community alive, make it successful, or rescue it from bad decisions.

The blockchain remembers. The market decides.

Your community's history remains on XRPL, whether your community becomes wildly successful or disappears from ordinary view.

We're not here to maintain a tidy database of everybody's adventures. We're here to make more adventures possible.

Your community. Your keys. Your responsibility.

Welcome aboard. Now go give somebody a reason to LEAP.

Continuing Operations

These are operating jobs, not a required sequence. Open the one you need.

1Recognition & Distribution

Your job is to recognize people and get LEAP out. The Operator decides what deserves recognition. Wild Ledger helps prepare the distribution. The authorized wallet approves it. XRPL preserves the result.

Use the recognition type that matches the human story:

  • Badge — recognition that may be earned repeatedly while it is active. Great Chat is the standard repeatable Badge available to established LEAP communities.
  • Event — recognition for participation in a particular event. A particular Event ID counts once for a member.
  • Challenge — recognition for completing a defined objective. A particular Challenge ID counts once for a member.

One award has one reason. A recognition award records one Badge, Event, or Challenge and a positive whole-number amount of LEAP.

Know when it counts. A direct Payment counts when LEAP is delivered. A Check does not add Earned LEAP merely because it was created; it counts when the member successfully cashes the Check and LEAP is delivered.

The Operator may recognize participation wherever the community actually lives: chat, a video, a comment thread, an event, a direct interaction, or an in-person meeting. Software can organize the opportunity and prepare the transaction. It does not decide whether a human contribution was worthy.

Recognition is not the same thing as ordinary distribution. Protocol-valid recognition adds Earned LEAP and recognition history. An ordinary LEAP transfer may move LEAP without creating a Badge, Event, Challenge, Great Chat, Earned LEAP, or rank credit.

2Participation

Create reasons for people to engage, then recognize what happens. Wild Ledger does not prescribe the campaign, venue, channel, or style.

A public opportunity answers “What can I do?” A member record answers “What have I done?” Keep those two jobs separate.

Community-defined Badges, Events, and Challenges have a lifecycle. CREATE puts a legitimate opportunity into use. END retires it without erasing recognition already earned. IGNORE quarantines an erroneous, test, invalid, or otherwise non-operational definition. These lifecycle actions manage the opportunity; they do not award LEAP to a member.

Conversation itself does not have to become permanent ledger state. A live room can be temporary while recognition earned there survives as durable XRPL history.

Where a community accepts voluntary XRP support, keep the two flows distinct: appreciation can flow inward in XRP; recognition flows outward in LEAP. Wild Ledger does not take custody of either participant's private keys or funds.

Keep the interaction moving. A good recognition workflow lets the Operator notice somebody, recognize the contribution, approve the wallet action, and return to the community.

3LEAP Inventory & Replenishment

Spread LEAP to earn more operating inventory. A new community begins with 1,000 LEAP of operating inventory.

Wild Ledger measures qualifying outward distribution with Spread Score. Each qualifying outside wallet can contribute no more than 20 lifetime Spread Score points to a community. This cap belongs to the community's permanent ledger history and cannot be reset.

When the community reaches 500 Spread Score points, it qualifies for another 1,000 LEAP of operating inventory.

Real outward distribution counts. Controlled wallets, circular movement, and manufactured spread do not. Returns reduce net spread rather than creating new reach.

Recognition and Spread Score answer different questions. Recognition asks, “What did this member earn?” Spread Score asks, “How broadly did this community distribute LEAP?” A transaction can affect both, but the records do not mean the same thing.

Show me the math

For each qualifying outside wallet, count the community's net qualifying outward LEAP, capped at 20 lifetime points for that wallet. Add those wallet contributions to produce the community's Spread Score.

Example: 10 qualifying outside wallets that each receive 20 net qualifying LEAP contribute 200 Spread Score points. Twenty-five such wallets can contribute 500 points. Sending more than 20 qualifying LEAP to one wallet may be useful recognition or distribution, but that wallet cannot contribute more than 20 lifetime Spread Score points.

The ledger is the history. The community's Spread Score and prior qualifying distribution remain in its permanent XRPL record. Neither can be reset by using a different wallet address.

4Operating Wallet Authority & Security

Your Community ID and your Community Operating Wallet are permanently bound together.

When a Wild Ledger community is established, its Community ID becomes permanently associated with one XRPL Operating Wallet address. That relationship lasts for the lifetime of the community.

The Operating Wallet cannot be replaced, rotated, reassigned, or transferred to another XRPL wallet address. Neither the community Operator nor the Wild Ledger Owner Wallet can change that relationship after establishment.

Your wallet address is the authority. Your wallet application is simply how you exercise it.

You may use a compatible wallet application, such as Bifrost, to access and sign transactions for your authorized Operating Wallet. Changing wallet applications does not change your community's authority, provided you continue using the same XRPL account.

Likewise, changing who controls the original wallet does not change the wallet's protocol identity.

Protect your Operating Wallet.

Wild Ledger does not possess your private keys or recovery credentials. If your Operating Wallet becomes inaccessible, Wild Ledger cannot recover it by assigning a replacement wallet. A lost or compromised wallet does not free the Community ID for reuse.

Your community and its history remain recorded on XRPL, but the authority to operate that community cannot be moved to another wallet address under the current LEAP Protocol.

The ledger preserves your history.

Previously validated community recognition, LEAP allocations, Spread Score, membership history, and other legitimate activity remain part of the community's permanent XRPL record.

Hiding a community changes its public visibility, not its existence or operating authority.

Your community. Your wallet. Your responsibility.

5Grooming Your Onboarding Checkbook

Outstanding onboarding Checks consume XRP reserves. Each Check issued by your Community Operating Wallet increases its XRP owner reserve requirement until that Check is successfully cashed or canceled.

Groom your Onboarding Checkbook periodically. Review outstanding membership requests and issued Checks that have not been cashed. When a Check has been sitting uncashed for too long, consider canceling it to release the associated XRP reserve.

Canceling a Check doesn't close the door on that person. They can submit another membership request and try again.

Regular grooming helps keep your Operating Wallet's XRP reserves available for ongoing community operations.

How Wild Ledger Works

Reference material for people who want to understand the machinery. You do not need to master this section to operate your community.

Ledger as Authority

Canonical state exists on XRPL. Wild Ledger interprets canonical state; it does not become its keeper.

A useful test is simple: if the Wild Ledger website disappeared, the ledger should still contain the durable facts Wild Ledger committed to the ledger.

Wild Ledger can reconstruct what happened by reading validated XRPL history in order and applying the protocol rules. The website is an interface and interpreter, not a second source of truth.

Human Recognition & Privacy

The Operator recognizes. Wild Ledger distributes. XRPL remembers.

Automate the scaffolding, not the humanity. Wild Ledger can present opportunities, prepare transactions, verify results, and reconstruct history. The human Operator decides when human participation deserves recognition.

We'd like to know less about you. Wild Ledger should ask for no more information than it needs for the immediate function. It does not need to build a permanent private profile merely because software can collect one.

Wild Ledger does not maintain its own database as a competing keeper of canonical community history. Where permanent record matters, the ledger carries the durable fact.

Wallet Roles & Provenance

Different wallets do different jobs. The Issuer defines and issues LEAP. The Distributor handles protocol-authorized distribution functions such as community allocations. The Wild Ledger Owner Wallet carries Wild Ledger owner authority. Each Community Operating Wallet speaks and acts for its own community.

Signer identity matters. Two transactions can use the same XRPL mechanism and still have different protocol meaning because they were signed by different authorized roles for different purposes.

That is why Wild Ledger verifies who signed, what asset is involved, where it is going, what the memo says, and which protocol rules apply instead of classifying a transaction by transaction type alone.

Protocol Statements & Community State

Not every community action transfers LEAP. After a community is established, its permanent Community Operating Wallet may publish canonical community-state statements through successful validated XRPL transactions whose public memos carry the applicable protocol statements.

Establishing a Community Operating Wallet is a one-time, permanent authority event: the permanent Wild Ledger Owner Wallet establishes the first Operating Wallet for a new Community ID, while the protocol also recognizes the one-time historical migration path for an already-established community. Neither kind of establishment permits later wallet replacement. The Owner Wallet is itself permanent.

Recognition lifecycle, CONFIG, and other legitimately updateable protocol state are interpreted from validated ledger evidence under their applicable rules. For state that is allowed to change, validated ledger order determines which valid value is current. These statements do not automatically mean that a member earned recognition or that LEAP moved; permanent wallet bindings are not updateable state values.

Progressive Disclosure & Ledger Trails

Wild Ledger does not hide Web3 from the human. It makes Web3 understandable to the human.

Ledger Trails make consequential operations observable: connect the wallet, verify the connection, prepare the action, ask the human to review and sign, submit it, wait for network validation, independently verify the result, and show completion.

The normal view can stay simple. The underlying evidence remains available when somebody wants to inspect it. This is progressive disclosure rather than concealment.

Wild Ledger does not remove complexity. It organizes access to it. A newcomer can follow plain-language checkpoints; an experienced Operator can move quickly; a learner or troubleshooter can open the evidence underneath.

The more consequential and permanent an action is, the less willing Wild Ledger should be to hide that action behind abstraction.

Tax Reporting

Wild Ledger can surface ledger facts. Valuation and tax treatment remain your responsibility.

1Transaction History & Timestamps

Use the validated XRPL record as the factual starting point. Wild Ledger can surface the transaction, asset and amount, relevant wallets, validated ledger timestamp, and the community context that Wild Ledger can reconstruct from the ledger.

Where XRP was received, the ledger can establish how much XRP moved and when the validated transaction occurred.

Wild Ledger does not determine the market value of XRP at that timestamp. It is not an exchange or a tax authority. The Operator or taxpayer can use the validated timestamp with the valuation source appropriate to their own reporting process.

Wild Ledger also does not decide whether a transaction is taxable, determine cost basis, or provide tax advice. Its job is to make the underlying ledger facts usable and exportable so the person responsible for the return—or their tax professional—can apply the appropriate rules.
